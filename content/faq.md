# Frequently asked questions

## What data counts?

Records your company created in the normal course of business, for example:

- CRM records and sales notes
- Support tickets and customer conversations
- Internal documents, wikis and SOPs
- Source code and commit history
- Project boards and task histories
- Invoices and operational records

Older, larger and well-organised sets tend to be most useful. Whether a specific set fits depends on what buyers need at the time.

## What about privacy and personal data?

Nothing is shared before a contract is signed. The contract sets out how personal data is removed or de-identified, who does it, and what the buyer may use the data for. You decide what's in scope, and you can say no at any stage before signing.

## Who pays, and what does it cost me?

Nothing. I'm paid a referral fee by the buyer. It costs you nothing. Any license fee for your data is agreed between you and the buyer and paid to you.

## How much is my data worth?

It depends on the data: type, volume, age, quality and current buyer demand. Some sets may earn a meaningful license fee; others may not be a fit. I'll tell you honestly after the first review, and I won't quote a number I can't stand behind.

## Do I have to sell my company?

No. You license a copy of the data. You keep the business, the systems and the original records.

## Can closed or liquidated companies take part?

Yes. For liquidators and insolvency practitioners, a company's data can be an extra asset to realise for creditors, at no cost to the estate and with minimal time required. The same rule applies: contract first, with de-identification agreed before anything is shared.
